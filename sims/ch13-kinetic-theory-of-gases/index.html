Molecules move in 3D; the view shows x and y (depth not drawn). They are hard spheres 0.6 nm across, drawn to scale. Real small molecules are about 0.3–0.4 nm; these are a little larger so collisions happen often enough to see. All collisions are elastic, and there are no other forces between molecules. When the molecules' own volume is more than a few percent of the box, measured pressure runs higher than the ideal gas law predicts. k = 1.38 × 10−23 J/K.

Try this

  1. Turn collisions on and press Start all at one speed. Watch the histogram as you play. What shape does it settle into, and how long does that take? Try it again with collisions off.
  2. Record a trial at 300 K, then at 600 K. By what factor did the pressure change? The average kinetic energy? vrms?
  3. At constant temperature, record trials with L = 20 nm and L = 40 nm. By what factor did the volume change? The pressure? Check that PV stays the same.
  4. Switch from helium to xenon at the same temperature. Which changes, the average kinetic energy or vrms? Why?
  5. Compare the difference between measured and predicted pressure with N = 10 and N = 300. Why does a real container of gas give a steady pressure?
  6. Set N = 300 and L = 10 nm. The molecules now fill several percent of the box. Is the measured pressure higher or lower than NkT/V? Why does the molecules' size matter?
